Already here

It writes the code. Drafts the contract. Reads the scan. Argues the case. On the benchmark, it is already superhuman.

And yet

The felt experience of using it has barely moved: you sit at a blank box and explain yourself, again, from scratch. The intelligence is extraordinary. The interface to it is a text field.

The gap

Between how capable the model is and how little of that capability reaches you on an ordinary day. It is not a modelling problem. It is a setup problem. You can close it in a day, with someone beside you.

A few things to know

Before you book.

Do we need API keys or a new subscription?

No. Level 1 uses whatever your team already uses. Levels 2 and 3 run on the Claude Code or Codex access your team already has. Your provider’s usage limits still apply. Local models cover voice and some text tasks with no account at all.

I’m not technical. Is this for me?

Yes. Most people in the room aren’t. Level 1 and Level 2 need no code at all. Level 3 is for anyone who wants to build the thing that runs their job; we write the seventy lines together.

Can we skip a level?

Yes. A team that prompts well every day starts at Level 2. A team with Claude Code already in the workflow starts at Level 3. Tell us where you are when you book.

Does our data leave our machines?

Switchboard runs locally. Context and data stay in folders you own. When an app calls a cloud model, that request goes to the provider you chose, and each app has to be granted access first. You watch the calls during the consent block.

Windows or Linux?

Not yet. Switchboard is a Mac app, macOS 13 or later on Apple Silicon. For on-site sessions we can pair a few Macs for a mixed team; tell us the mix when you book.

What should we bring?

A laptop, a sign-in for Claude Code or Codex for Levels 2 and 3, and one real project: a brand, a product, a client, a weekly job.

Who coaches?

Sameep, and the people building Switchboard at The Last Prompt. We run our own company on these tools every day. You get the same people beside you, not a trainer reading slides.

How many people in the room?

Small. An open day is a handful of founders, operators and creatives, one coach. 1:1 is you and the coach.

What happens after the day?

You keep a group with your coach for the weeks after. Ask when something breaks, send what you built, and tell us when you’re ready for the next level.
