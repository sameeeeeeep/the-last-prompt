  1. 01PROMPTING

    Prompt for a real task.

    Write a brief that gives AI the task, background, constraints and output format. Use examples, follow-up questions and revision to get a result you can use.

    What you practise

    • Structure a prompt: task, context, examples, constraints and format.
    • Work with text, supplied documents and images.
    • Compare two outputs, check facts and save the instructions that work.
    YOUR PRACTICAL ASSIGNMENT

    Prepare for a specific role.

    +

    Bring a job description and your existing experience. Build prompts for a skills-gap review, interview rehearsal and a project brief.

    A saved prompt set tied to one role and a practical preparation plan.

  2. 02RESEARCH & CONTEXT

    Build a reliable knowledge base.

    Work from source material: a syllabus, project folder, product information or public document. Give AI the context it needs and make it show where its answers come from.

    What you practise

    • Organise a small source pack and project brief.
    • Extract, compare and summarise information from those sources.
    • Save reusable context and distinguish evidence from an unsupported answer.
    YOUR PRACTICAL ASSIGNMENT

    Research your project before building.

    +

    Use your course references and supplied sources to compare possible solutions to a campus or local-service problem. Record the evidence and unanswered questions.

    A source-backed project brief you can explain to a reviewer.

    YOU LEAVE WITH

    A source pack and checked research brief

  3. 03CONTENT & COMMUNICATION

    Make documents, slides and learning material.

    Turn your brief and source pack into a finished piece of communication. Shape the writing, structure, visuals and language for the people who will use it.

    What you practise

    • Create a structured document, presentation or digital catalogue.
    • Adapt material into a regional language or bilingual format.
    • Revise the copy and layout; check meaning and claims before sharing.
    YOUR PRACTICAL ASSIGNMENT

    Make a bilingual project presentation.

    +

    Turn your research into a short slide deck and one-page explainer in English and the cohort’s preferred language. Check both against the same source pack.

    An editable presentation and bilingual explainer.

  4. 04DATA & SPREADSHEETS

    Make a spreadsheet you can explain.

    Use AI to organise data, help write formulas and prepare a chart. Check the result against the original numbers and use it to answer a specific question.

    What you practise

    • Clean a small table and identify missing or inconsistent values.
    • Build and check formulas for totals, costs and comparisons.
    • Create a chart and explain the assumptions behind the result.
    YOUR PRACTICAL ASSIGNMENT

    Cost your first version.

    +

    Create a project budget in rupees from supplied prices. Use formulas to compare two approaches and check the totals yourself.

    A working budget, comparison chart and explanation of the assumptions.

  5. 05WEBSITES & TOOLS

    Build a working first version.

    Take a small problem from idea to a usable website or tool. Use AI to build, test and revise it, then learn how to make a change yourself.

    What you practise

    • Define the user, essential features and a simple user journey.
    • Build a page or tool with the agreed AI-assisted environment.
    • Test links, forms and sample inputs; fix issues and document the setup.
    YOUR PRACTICAL ASSIGNMENT

    Build the project you described.

    +

    Make a local-service page, campus resource finder or small study tool. Test it with a peer and make one change without the trainer doing it for you.

    A functioning prototype with a short setup guide.

  6. 06AGENTS & WORKFLOWS

    Build a process that runs again.

    Combine instructions, context and tools for one repeatable job. Run it on more than one input, add human review points and demonstrate the complete workflow.

    What you practise

    • Map the trigger, inputs, tools, outputs and approval steps.
    • Connect approved resources and build one repeatable workflow.
    • Compare the result with a direct prompt; test failures and explain the limits.
    YOUR PRACTICAL ASSIGNMENT

    Run a project workflow end to end.

    +

    Connect a brief, task plan and progress summary using the tools agreed for the cohort. Try a second project and review the output before sharing it.

    A reusable workflow, a live demonstration and notes on where review is needed.

Planning an
institutional programme.

Delivery, assessment, technology and engagement details for colleges, public bodies and programme partners.

How is a programme designed for our institution?

We begin with the audience, their current skill level and the work they need to do. The programme outline identifies learning objectives, selected modules, practical assignments, delivery format and expected outputs. Your institution reviews the scope before scheduling.

What delivery formats and cohort sizes are available?

Programmes can be scoped for a campus or training centre, a pilot within an existing initiative, or a sequence of cohorts across a network. Session length, group size and facilitator requirements depend on the level of practical support needed, language and available facilities.

What prior experience do participants need?

Foundation modules assume no coding experience. A basic ability to use a browser and work with digital files is helpful. Advanced building and automation modules require an appropriate starting level and tool setup; these are established during programme planning.

How are learning outcomes assessed?

Participants complete practical assignments and demonstrate their work. Assessment can cover task completion, accuracy, the ability to explain decisions and the ability to repeat the process independently. The assessment criteria and any institution-level reporting are defined in the programme scope.

Can delivery and materials support regional languages?

Language requirements are captured at the scoping stage. Facilitation, bilingual materials and regional-language exercises are planned around the cohort, with available language support confirmed in the proposal. Translated outputs are reviewed for meaning and local usage.

What technology and access should the institution provide?

The programme plan lists required devices, connectivity, software and account access. Foundation sessions can use browser-based tools on existing laptops. Any specialist setup, provider subscription or optional Switchboard lab requirement is identified before delivery.

How are institutional data and participant work handled?

Practical work uses institution-approved material, public sources or demonstration datasets. Tool access and permitted data use are established before the programme. If a cloud AI provider is used, the relevant inputs are processed under that provider’s terms and settings; these requirements should align with the institution’s policies.

Is certification included?

Assessment-based certification from The Last Prompt can be included in the agreed scope. The certificate records the level and work demonstrated. It is a provider-issued certificate and does not confer university credit or government accreditation.

What do participants retain after the programme?

Participants retain the prompts, editable material, project files and workflow instructions produced during the programme. Ongoing support, refresher sessions and trainer handover requirements can be specified as part of the engagement.

Who delivers the programme, and how do we request a proposal?

Programmes are led by The Last Prompt’s practitioners, including Sameep and the team building Switchboard. Share your audience, location, preferred languages, cohort size and objectives through the enquiry form. The proposal sets out delivery responsibilities, scope, schedule, fees and any technology requirements.
