What AI literacy means in practice

Use it. Question it.
Know what you’re doing.

These four abilities form the foundation of our courses and workshops. You practise with real inputs, inspect what comes back and explain the choices you make.

01 / UNDERSTAND AI

What is the model actually doing?

Language models, AI applications, common capabilities and why a fluent answer can still be wrong.

TRY IT YOURSELF

Ask the same factual question in two ways. Compare the answers with a reliable source.

What you take awayExplain what you can trust, what needs checking and why.

02 / ASK CLEARLY

Write a prompt that gives useful direction.

The task, context, source material, constraints and output format. Use follow-up questions to improve a response.

TRY IT YOURSELF

Turn rough meeting notes into decisions and action items. Tell AI to flag missing owners and deadlines.

What you take awayA reusable prompt with an output you have checked.

03 / CHECK THE ANSWER

Find what the model missed or invented.

Verify claims and citations, compare an answer with its source, and recognise uncertainty and misleading confidence.

TRY IT YOURSELF

Use AI to read a public report. Trace the key claims back to specific passages and correct unsupported statements.

What you take awayAn annotated answer showing what you kept, corrected and rejected.

04 / USE IT RESPONSIBLY

Decide what to share and when to stop.

Personal and confidential information, biased outputs, appropriate disclosure and tasks that need human judgement.

TRY IT YOURSELF

Review a sample document before uploading it. Remove sensitive fields and explain what should stay out of the tool.

What you take awayA checklist for choosing inputs and reviewing outputs before use.

A short workshop introduces these habits. Longer programmes give you time to practise them across different tasks, get feedback and demonstrate them independently.

What is free, what the workshops cover and what certification recognises.

What is included in AI literacy?

The foundations cover what AI can and cannot do, writing useful prompts, checking answers and handling information appropriately. Participants practise on documents, questions and decisions rather than only watching demonstrations.

Are the assessment and basic courses free?

Yes. The introductory AI literacy assessment and basic courses are planned as free learning offers. They are currently marked coming soon. Offline workshops and certificate programmes are separate engagements, with scope and fees confirmed in a proposal.

How does a 3-hour workshop differ from a 3-month programme?

A 3-hour workshop introduces the core concepts and a guided exercise. A longer programme adds repeated practice, assignments, feedback and an independent assessment. The proposal specifies contact hours, practice time and the skills to be assessed. Completing the full seven-level engineering pathway is a separate scope decision.

Do participants need coding experience?

No coding experience is needed for the AI literacy foundation. Participants should be able to use a browser and digital files; we discuss any support they need before delivery. The optional engineering pathway introduces APIs, configuration and code at the relevant levels.

What does the AI literacy certificate recognise?

Our certificate recognises the programme and skills demonstrated in its practical assessment. Learners must meet the agreed completion and passing criteria; attendance alone is not evidence of proficiency. It is a provider-issued certificate, not a government accreditation or university qualification.

Is the free assessment the certification exam?

No. The free assessment is intended to identify a learner’s starting point and learning gaps. Certification uses a separate practical assessment within an agreed programme, including independent task completion, verification and an explanation of the learner’s decisions.

Where do offline workshops take place?

We plan in-person delivery at the institution’s campus, office or training centre. Cohort size, facilitation, devices, connectivity, accounts and venue arrangements are confirmed before scheduling. A pilot can be followed by further cohorts across a network.

Can delivery support regional languages?

We agree the languages required for facilitation, explanations and supporting material during scoping. Participants can discuss concepts in their preferred language while learning the terms used in AI tools. Available language support is confirmed in the proposal.

How are institutional data and learner work handled?

Exercises use public sources, demonstration datasets or institution-approved material. We agree permitted data use before delivery, including what must not be entered into cloud tools. Provider terms and settings are considered alongside the institution’s policies.

How can an institution request a programme?

Share the audience, location, preferred languages, cohort size and time available through the enquiry form. We will scope the learning objectives, delivery, practice, assessment, fees and technology requirements in a proposal for your institution to review.
